§1 · The Hook

It's like NieR: Automata's bullet hell, but the arena is shared — you and every other avatar in the plaza survive the same pattern together.

§2 · First Session — as "you"

You spawn on a neon-lit walkway ringing a sunken arena. Below, glowing orbs bloom outward in slow spirals, and a handful of avatars weave between them. A pulsing ring on the floor marks the drop-in point. [HYPOTHESIS → H1-04]

You drop in as a new wave starts — wide, slow arcs. You sidestep the first one; a low chime marks the near miss. Then an orb flashes blue as it closes on you — you press, and it ricochets back through the pattern, punching a hole the avatar beside you dives through.

Wave by wave the pattern tightens. You survive your first full round of three waves; the arena rim lights up with the next round's name and a preview of its pattern — visibly denser.

Why the first repetition feels good — measured, not hoped: the verb held with no score, reward or goal attached — 3 voluntary greybox waves, quit by choice (H1-01 survived). Two load-bearing conditions are part of the verb: the deflect is scarce (1.5 s cooldown) and the telegraph is honest (exactly one bullet glows blue — the one E sends back). Deflect timing is fair: 12/13 intended deflects (92% vs ≥70% bar), landings predictable before the press (H1-02 validated). Genre density runs: 112 avg render FPS at 150 simultaneous projectiles, zero hiccups (H1-03 validated). Both mobile pending. Whether the verb pulls players who didn't invent it is H1-04 — deferred to v1's external testers.

§7 · World, Look & Story

Neo-Tokyo's grid is haunted by digital yokai, and each round the arena summons one — its bullet pattern is that spirit's fury given light. You survive the haunting together with everyone in the pit; deflecting a bullet throws the yokai's own fury back at it.

Visual direction: Ghostwire: Tokyo (rain-slick neon shrine streets) · NieR: Automata's glowing-orb bullet language · Akira's Neo-Tokyo night palette. Stylized PBR, baked lighting. [agent-decided — owner named "cyberpunk yokai"; references chosen to match]

§8 · Comparables

What worked3D bullet-hell patterns — slow, fat, glowing orbs readable in 3D space — fused with a Japanese sci-fi/cyberpunk mood.
What didn'tCombat depth and camera precision far beyond a small team's scope; not what made the patterns memorable.
What we do differentlyKeep NieR's bullet language, strip its combat: no shooting, no combos — one shared arena, one timed aimed deflect that visibly helps everyone. Short sessions in a social world, not a 30-hour solo campaign.
