YOKAI DANMAKU

A shared bullet-hell arena in haunted neo-Tokyo: every avatar survives the same yokai's pattern together, and timed deflects are the only way to banish it.

Creator Success proposal · stage 2 (pitch) · Pravus, solo, ~20h/week · requested round: v1
Status: playable core-loop greybox in SDK7 — deflect 92% intended-hit, 150 projectiles @ 112 fps, anti-camping machine-verified · TBD: public link before submission

1 · The Hook

It's like NieR: Automata's bullet hell, but the arena is shared — you and every other avatar in the plaza survive the same pattern together.

2 · First Session

You spawn on a neon walkway ringing a sunken arena — below, glowing orbs bloom in slow spirals (the haunting never sleeps, even over an empty pit). You drop in as a wave starts; sidestep, near-miss chime. An orb flashes blue — you press, and it ricochets back, punching a hole the avatar beside you dives through. Every deflect chips the yokai's bar; three waves in it shatters and the rim previews the next round — visibly denser.

The last thing you see before leaving: this week's haunting board catching your name — your deepest round as a mark, the Sunday countdown ticking beside it. It won't survive tonight's crowd unless you come back.

3 · Core Loop

One wave ≈ 40 s (measured) · one round ≈ 2–2.5 min · session = collective run to the wipe, target median 8–15 min

VerbWhat you doWhy again
READWatch the pattern, find the gapEvery wave draws a new pattern
WEAVEThread your avatar throughGaps sweep from a random start every volley — no fixed spot survives (measured)
DEFLECTTimed, camera-aimed ricochetPunches a hole for everyone; the only damage to the yokai
SURVIVEOutlast the waveEmpty the bar → banish → a denser yokai
Anti-camping is law, not a hazard: H2-01 proved the bar makes camping correct wherever a pattern permits it; the redesigned pattern law (undeflectable wall volleys, sweeping twin entrances) refuses it — zero majority-camped rounds banished, machine-logged. Standing law: every yokai's every wave must carry an anti-camping element.

10th repetition: other players (crowd = the variety — unmeasured, v1), rising difficulty, skill expression. Solo floor held: 15 voluntary waves / 4 runs, quit by choice. Knockout = spectate one wave from the walkway; full wipe resets the haunting to round 1.

4 · Why Players Come Back

"Last night's deepest round — we got to round 7 — is on this week's haunting board with my name on it, and it will not survive tonight's crowd unless I come defend it."

  • Hook 1 — Weekly haunting board. Brackets of 20, remainder merges (below 40 weekly placers: one board of everyone — sized by arithmetic to DCL-real traffic). Median player has ~7 beatable marks within 2 rounds. Known fact: #1 is usually a runaway — the drama is mid-table defense, never "beat the #1". Resets Sunday 00:00 UTC.
  • Hook 2 — The Great Haunting. Friday 20:00 UTC, one automated mega-yokai template with its own dedicated model. The calendar is the platform's only notification system.
  • Hook 3 — The yokai scrapbook. Silhouettes fill permanently when you stand through a banishment. Pacing measured: no wall (last page ~3 sessions) — and honestly a fast book at v1 scale; its long pull is the mega page + content drops.

Day 7: league tier over your head, lifetime best, 1–2 Great Haunting charms on your avatar, scrapbook filling. Long-term: the Exorcist wearable — 4 Great Haunting banishments, calendar-gated one month, charm-visible during the climb (mint at v2; charms ship in v1).

5 · Social by Design

Better with others

Solo, your knockout is the run's death; with others the run survives your mistakes. Deflects stack — deep rounds are crowd-gated. Every deflect is visible help in the pattern itself.

Quiet hour

The haunting runs over an empty pit; the rim shows last night's names and the Friday countdown. Solo floor is measured fun. Any second player who drops in is instantly in your run — the shared arena is the matchmaking.

Bystander

The whole loop is world-space. Five seconds of watching teaches both verbs; knocked-out players are a manufactured audience, one wave from playing again. Bullets never reach the walkway; the wall volley ejects AFK bodies.

The clip

The last-one-standing banish: walkway full of invested spectators, one player lands the final deflect, the yokai shatters skyward over live action (no-pause law stands). One shared spectacle in v1.

7 · World & Look

Neo-Tokyo's grid is haunted by digital yokai; each round's bullet pattern is that spirit's fury given light. Ghostwire: Tokyo × NieR's glowing-orb bullet language × Akira's night palette — stylized PBR, baked lighting.
